## Why this policy exists

Aixle Insights is free and open source under the [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE),
and §6 of that license grants **no trademark rights**:

> This License does not grant permission to use the trade names, trademarks,
> service marks, or product names of the Licensor […]

The code is yours to use, modify, and redistribute. The name is how users know
which software came from this project, so it is handled separately. This policy
explains what you may do with the Marks without asking, what needs written
permission, and how to ask.

Nothing here restricts your rights under the Apache License with respect to the
source code itself.

## What you may do without asking

- **Refer to the project by name.** Write about Aixle Insights, blog, present,
  teach, review, or compare it. Accurate, descriptive references ("nominative
  use") need no permission.
- **State compatibility or integration.** "Works with Aixle Insights",
  "exports to Aixle Insights", "an Aixle Insights connector" — as long as the
  statement is true and does not suggest that Dualboot built or endorsed your
  product.
- **Redistribute unmodified copies** of the project under its own name, keeping
  [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) and [`NOTICE`](NOTICE) intact.
- **Say that your work is derived from Aixle Insights.** A fork may factually
  describe its origin — "based on Aixle Insights", "a fork of Aixle Insights" —
  in prose, documentation, and release notes.
- **Use the name inside your own organization**, for internal deployments,
  documentation, dashboards, and training materials.

## What needs written permission

- Naming a **modified version, fork, or derivative product** "Aixle",
  "Aixle Insights", or anything confusingly similar (including compounds and
  misspellings such as "Aixle Pro", "Aixly", "AixleOps").
- Using the Marks in your **company name, product name, app name, domain name,
  package name, or social media handle**.
- Using the **logo** as your own logo, app icon, or favicon, or incorporating it
  into another mark.
- Using the Marks on **merchandise** or in paid advertising.
- Offering a **commercial hosted or managed service** under the Aixle name.
- Any use that states or implies **sponsorship, endorsement, affiliation, or
  official status**.

## Rules that always apply

- **Do not alter the Marks.** Keep the spelling and capitalization — "Aixle",
  "Aixle Insights". No abbreviations, translations, plurals, or possessives of
  the Marks themselves.
- **Do not modify the logo** — no recoloring, stretching, cropping, rotating, or
  combining it with other graphics.
- **Do not create confusion.** Your name, branding, and look and feel must be
  distinct enough that users can tell your product from ours.
- **Do not register** the Marks, confusingly similar marks, or corresponding
  domain names anywhere in the world.
- **Attribute when you use them.** Where practical, include: *"Aixle and Aixle
  Insights are trademarks of Dualboot Partners, LLC."*

## Forks and modified versions

Forking is welcome — that is what the Apache License is for. To keep users from
being misled about who is responsible for what:

1. **Give your fork its own name** and its own logo. You may describe it as
   "based on Aixle Insights" but the product itself must not be called Aixle
   Insights.
2. **Replace Aixle branding in the UI and packaging** — the logo, the app title,
   the npm package name.
3. **Keep the license files** — `LICENSE`, `NOTICE`, and the attribution they
   contain. Removing them is a license violation, not a trademark question.
4. **Be clear about support.** Make it obvious that Dualboot does not maintain
   or support your fork.

If your fork stays a faithful, unmodified redistribution — for example a distro
package or a mirror — you may keep the original name and branding.

## Requesting permission or reporting misuse

For permission requests, questions about a borderline use, or reports of
trademark misuse, write to `legal.insights@aixle.com`. Please do not use the
public issue tracker for legal correspondence.

We are generally happy to say yes to community uses that keep the origin of the
software clear.
